H–R Diagram ×

What this shows

Plot stars by surface temperature (hot on the left, by tradition) and luminosity, and they don’t scatter randomly — they fall into a few groups. About 90% sit on the main sequence, the diagonal strip where a star spends its long hydrogen-burning life. Cool but bright stars in the upper right must be huge: they are giants and supergiants. Hot but faint stars in the lower left are tiny, dense white dwarfs — dead stellar cores. Because L = 4πR²σT⁴, position on this diagram tells you a star’s radius.

How to use

Drop one of the named stars, drag the sliders, or click anywhere on the diagram. The dashed diagonals are lines of constant radius. Watch where your star lands: on the main sequence you also get a rough mass and lifetime; off it, the label tells you what kind of object has that temperature and luminosity.

Relevant relationships
luminosity from radius and temperature — the constant-R diagonals.
main-sequence mass–luminosity relation (rough).
main-sequence lifetime — massive stars burn out fast.
