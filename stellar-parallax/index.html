Stellar Parallax ×

What this shows

Hold up a finger and blink between eyes — it jumps against the background. Do the same with a nearby star using the two ends of Earth’s orbit (six months apart, 2 AU apart) as your two eyes, and the star traces a tiny ellipse against the far more distant background stars over a year. Half the side-to-side swing is the parallax angle p. The distance in parsecs is simply 1 / p with p in arcseconds — that is what “parsec” means. This is the one direct rung on the whole cosmic distance ladder.

How to use

Pick a star or set a distance, then run the year. The top view shows Earth’s orbit and the sight line to the star swinging back and forth; the lower panel is the star’s apparent path on the sky. Move the star farther away and the wobble — and the measurable parallax — shrinks toward nothing.

Relevant relationships
distance in parsecs = 1 over the parallax angle in arcseconds.
the small-angle geometry it comes from: baseline 1 AU, angle p.
1 parsec in everyday units — about 3.26 light-years.

Worked example — the distance to 61 Cygni ×

In 1838 Bessel measured a parallax of 0.314 arcseconds for 61 Cygni — the first stellar distance ever found. How far away is it?

1 · The parsec relation
2 · In light-years
3 · Interpret
About 10.4 light-years — the modern value is 11.4 ly. An angle of 0.3″ is the width of a coin seen from ~15 km, which is why no one managed it before precise telescopes.
