Exoplanet Transit ×

What this shows

If a planet’s orbit is lined up edge-on to us, once per orbit it crosses in front of its star and blocks a sliver of light. The star dims by the ratio of the two discs’ areas — typically a fraction of a percent — then recovers. Measure the depth and you get the planet’s size; measure the spacing of the dips and you get its orbital period; the shape of the ingress and egress tells you how centrally it crossed. This is how most known exoplanets were found.

How to use

Set a planet size and orbit and hit Play. The upper strip is the planet crossing the star’s disc; the lower panel builds the light curve. Grow the planet and the dip deepens as the square of its radius. Slide the impact parameter up toward 1 and the crossing gets shorter and more V-shaped, until past ~1 there is no transit at all.

Relevant relationships
transit depth = the disc-area ratio.
period from Kepler’s third law (star mass in solar units).
transit duration — longer for a big star, a close orbit, and a central crossing.
