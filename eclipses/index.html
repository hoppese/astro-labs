Eclipses

What this shows

An eclipse is one body’s shadow falling on another. A solar eclipse needs a new Moon whose shadow reaches Earth; a lunar eclipse needs a full Moon that enters Earth’s shadow. The Moon’s orbit is tilted 5°, so it works only when a new or full Moon comes near a node, where the orbit crosses the Earth–Sun plane. That happens in two “seasons” a year, about 173 days apart.

A solar eclipse depends on where you stand: total on a narrow path, partial around it, nothing elsewhere. A lunar eclipse looks the same to everyone who has the Moon up.

What is simplified

Build your own: the Moon follows an oval (Kepler) orbit tilted 5.145° with its nodes turning 19.35° a year, plus its largest solar perturbation; the sundial and clock agree at the moment of new or full Moon (no equation of time). Real eclipses: Sun and Moon from astronomy-engine, checked against its own eclipse search. Both: a slightly flattened Earth with no mountains, a smooth round Moon (no lunar valleys), no bending of light at the horizon beyond the usual 0.83° for rising and setting. Earth’s shadow is enlarged 81 km for its atmosphere. From space: lengths along the sunlight squeezed 8×. Month: tilt and body sizes both 4× too big, so within a few hours of an eclipse the drawn shadow can look close when it still misses; the labels use the true geometry. Year: not to scale.

Worked example: is this new Moon a solar eclipse?

A new Moon comes 3° past the ascending node, when the Moon is 362,000 km away. Is the Sun eclipsed anywhere on Earth, and what kind of eclipse is it?

2 · How much room is there?
Seen from different places on Earth the Moon shifts by up to this much, so the Moon’s center can be put right on the Sun’s from somewhere if β is under about 1°. A partial eclipse needs only the edges to touch: 1.01° + 0.27° + 0.27° ≈ 1.55°.
3 · Total or annular?
That is more than the Sun’s 0.266° (average), so the Moon covers it: a total eclipse along a narrow path, partial around it. It passes north of the middle of Earth, because the Moon is north of the plane.
4 · How far north?
The shadow’s axis passes about a quarter of Earth’s radius from the center. (The sim gives nearly the same at closest approach.) Try it: angle +3.0°, ascending node, 362,000 km.
