What this shows

A star’s entire life, traced as a path across the H–R diagram. It starts as a cool, contracting protostar, settles onto the main sequence where it spends ~90% of its life fusing hydrogen, then — when the core hydrogen runs out — swells into a red giant. What happens next depends almost entirely on the starting mass: a Sun-like star puffs off a planetary nebula and leaves a white dwarf; a star above ~8 M☉ ends in a core-collapse supernova, leaving a neutron star or black hole. Below ~0.08 M☉ the core never ignites at all — a brown dwarf.

How to use

Set a starting mass and drag the life-stage slider (or step through the stages). The purple path is the portion of the track the star has travelled so far; the dashed line is the main sequence for reference. Watch how a massive star tears through its whole life in a few million years while a red dwarf’s main-sequence phase outlasts the current age of the universe.
