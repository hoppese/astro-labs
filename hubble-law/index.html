Hubble’s Law ×

What this shows

Almost every galaxy’s light is redshifted, and the more distant ones are redshifted more — in exact proportion to their distance. That’s not because we’re at the centre of an explosion; it’s because space itself is expanding, so every galaxy sees every other one receding, faster the farther away it is. Run it backwards and everything meets at one moment: the Big Bang, roughly 1/H₀ ago.

How to use

Hit Run the expansion: watch the galaxies drift apart, each with a velocity arrow proportional to its distance. The graph plots velocity against distance — a straight line whose slope is H₀. Click a different galaxy to re-centre on it: same straight line, same slope. Change H₀ and the implied age of the universe changes with it.

Worked example — distance to a galaxy from its redshift ×

A galaxy’s spectral lines are shifted so that Δλ/λ = 0.02. Taking H₀ = 70 km/s/Mpc, how far away is it, and what does H₀ imply for the age of the universe?

1 · Redshift → speed
2 · Speed → distance
3 · Age ≈ 1/H₀
