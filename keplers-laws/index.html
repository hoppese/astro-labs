Kepler’s Laws

What this shows

First law: planets orbit on ellipses with the Sun at one focus — not the centre. Second law: the line from the Sun to the planet sweeps out equal areas in equal times, so the planet races through perihelion and crawls through aphelion. Third law: the orbital period squared equals the semi-major axis cubed (in years and AU), so a planet twice as far out takes about 2.8× as long to go round.

How to use

Stretch the orbit with the eccentricity slider and hit Play. The orbit is cut into wedges at equal steps of time (choose 6, 8, 12 or 24 per orbit). Every wedge, shaded or not, takes the same time, P/N, and has the same area; the shading only alternates so you can tell neighbours apart. The Results list gives the time and area of the wedge the planet is in. The background grid is in AU, centred on the Sun, with a scale bar and the planets’ orbits as yardsticks: as you raise the semi-major axis the orbit grows across the grid, and when it no longer fits the view zooms out a step (watch the grid numbers and scale bar change). The green arrow is the planet’s velocity (longer = faster), and the lower strip is its speed over one orbit. Load a comet like Halley to see the effect at the extreme.
