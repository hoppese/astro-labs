Kepler’s Laws ×

What this shows

First law: planets orbit on ellipses with the Sun at one focus — not the centre. Second law: the line from the Sun to the planet sweeps out equal areas in equal times, so the planet races through perihelion and crawls through aphelion. Third law: the orbital period squared equals the semi-major axis cubed (in years and AU), so a planet twice as far out takes about 2.8× as long to go round.

How to use

Stretch the orbit with the eccentricity slider and hit Play. The shaded wedges each cover the same slice of time — watch them stay equal in area even as their shape changes. The lower strip is the planet’s speed over one orbit. Load a comet like Halley to see the effect at the extreme.
