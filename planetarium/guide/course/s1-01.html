By the end of class, students can

  • say what separates astronomy from astrology: a claim you can test against the sky, and what happens when you test astrology’s;
  • name the course’s thread: how we measure distances we can never travel (the cosmic distance ladder), starting this semester with the Earth, the Moon, the Sun and the planets;
  • feel the scale: the Earth, the Moon’s distance, the Sun’s, the nearest star, in a few steps of powers of ten;
  • find the planetarium, its labs, and the lab guides.

Before class

  • Update Syllabus.key (2022 version): it still names Comins & Kaufmann and the old Thursday scanned-PDF homework. Replace with OpenStax Astronomy 2e, Moodle kickoffs and homework, planetarium activities.
  • Have the planetarium open on the projector at tonight’s sky from Richmond, and a list of the class’s birthdays’ zodiac signs ready to collect (a show of hands works).
  • No kickoff today.

The 50 minutes

0–8Open with astrology (Ideas.txt). “What’s your sign?” Ask who knows theirs; ask what it is supposed to mean (the Sun was “in” that constellation when you were born). Then show it: set the planetarium to a student’s birthday and select the Sun. In most cases the Sun is in the previous sign’s constellation. Leave the why hanging (it is precession, and the class will measure it later in the semester).
8–15Astronomy vs. astrology. Both watch the same sky; one makes predictions that can fail. A short think-pair-share: “How would you test a horoscope?” Collect two or three ideas; mention the blind tests that have been done.
15–30The syllabus, briefly (Syllabus.key): kickoffs, homework, activities are planetarium labs, quizzes, midterm, final. The learning goals, including “have a favorite star” and “pay more attention to the Sun, the Moon and the stars”.
30–43The scale of things, and the plan. A powers-of-ten walk: the Earth (12,700 km), the Moon (about 30 Earths away), the Sun (about 400 times farther), the nearest star (about 270,000 times farther again). Then the question for the whole course: how could anyone know these numbers? This semester answers it out to the edge of the solar system, the way it was first done, by students doing the measurements themselves.
43–50Meet the planetarium. Open it from the course page; show search, time controls, the Labs button and a lab guide. Assign: open it at home tonight, find the Moon, and look for it outside.

Watch for

  • Students who take astrology seriously: frame the test as a question about evidence, not a put-down; the sign/constellation mismatch is a fact about the sky either way.
  • “Astronomer” and “astrologer” used interchangeably: name the difference once, then use the words carefully all term.
