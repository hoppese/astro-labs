By the end of class, students can

  • name the course’s thread: how we measure distances we can never travel (the cosmic distance ladder), starting this semester with the Earth, the Moon, the Sun and the planets;
  • feel the scale: the Earth, the Moon’s distance, the Sun’s, the nearest star, in a few steps of powers of ten;
  • find the planetarium, its labs, and the lab guides.

Before class

  • Update Syllabus.key (2022 version): it still names Comins & Kaufmann and the old Thursday scanned-PDF homework. Replace with OpenStax Astronomy 2e, Moodle kickoffs and homework, planetarium activities.
  • Have the planetarium open on the projector at tonight’s sky from Richmond. Index cards for the estimates.
  • No kickoff today.

The 50 minutes

0–8Estimate first. On an index card, each student guesses: how far away is the Moon? the Sun? the nearest star other than the Sun? (Any units.) Collect the cards; read out the range of guesses, which is always enormous.
8–15How would you find out? Think-pair-share: “Nobody has ever been to the Sun. How could anyone know how far away it is?” Collect two or three ideas and keep them on the board: some of them are how it was really done, and the class will do it.
15–30The syllabus, briefly (Syllabus.key): kickoffs, homework, activities are planetarium labs, quizzes, midterm, final. The learning goals, including “have a favorite star” and “pay more attention to the Sun, the Moon and the stars”.
30–43The scale of things, and the plan. A powers-of-ten walk: the Earth (12,700 km), the Moon (about 30 Earths away), the Sun (about 400 times farther), the nearest star (about 270,000 times farther again). Then the question for the whole course: how could anyone know these numbers? This semester answers it out to the edge of the solar system, the way it was first done, by students doing the measurements themselves. Hand back the range of guesses from the cards.
43–50Meet the planetarium. Open it from the course page; show search, time controls, the Labs button and a lab guide. Assign: open it at home tonight, find the Moon, and look for it outside.

Watch for

  • Guesses for the Sun and the stars that are too small by many powers of ten: that is the point of the opening; come back to the cards once the scale walk is done.
  • Students who think the stars are “just beyond” the planets: the scale walk’s last step (about 270,000 times the Sun’s distance) is where to slow down.
