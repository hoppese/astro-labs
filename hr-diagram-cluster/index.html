Cluster Ages ×

What this shows

Every star in a cluster formed from the same cloud at essentially the same time, so a cluster is a set of stars of one age but a whole range of masses. The most massive stars burn out fastest, so as the cluster ages the top of the main sequence “peels away” — stars there swell into red giants and then fade to white dwarfs. The point where stars are just now leaving the main sequence is the turnoff, and the main-sequence lifetime of a star at the turnoff equals the cluster’s age. Fit the turnoff, get the age.

How to use

Drag the age slider (or hit Age the cluster) and watch the turnoff point slide down the main sequence while red giants pile up in the upper right. Load a real cluster to see its turnoff: the Pleiades still has hot blue O/B stars, so it’s young; M13’s turnoff is down at a Sun-like mass, so it’s ~12 billion years old.
