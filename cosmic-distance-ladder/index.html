What this shows

No single method reaches from the Moon to the edge of the observable universe. Instead astronomers use a ladder: a technique that works up close is used to calibrate one that reaches a bit farther, which calibrates the next, and so on. Radar and parallax are the only direct geometry; everything beyond depends on “standard candles” whose true brightness is fixed by a rung below. A shift in a lower rung ripples all the way up.

How to use

Drag the distance slider or pick an object. The ladder shows which methods can reach that far; the active one is highlighted, and the panel below traces the chain of calibration back to direct geometry. Notice how, far out, the chain is long — and every link adds uncertainty.

The rungs
Radar ranging — bounce a radio pulse off a planet; distance = c × (round-trip time)/2. Solar-system only.
Parallax — the star’s yearly shift against the background; d[pc] = 1/p[″]. Gaia reaches several kpc.
Main-sequence fitting — match a cluster’s main sequence to a calibrated one; the vertical offset gives the distance.
Cepheid variables — pulsation period fixes the true luminosity; compare with apparent brightness. Good to ~30 Mpc.
Type Ia supernovae — explode with a near-standard peak luminosity; visible across billions of parsecs.
Hubble’s law — measure the redshift, divide by H₀. The only tool for the most distant galaxies.
