What this shows

Most of the time an outer planet drifts slowly eastward against the background stars. But around opposition — when Earth, on its faster inside track, catches up and passes it — the planet appears to stop, back up westward for a few weeks, then resume. Nothing about the planet changes; it is entirely a line-of-sight effect of Earth overtaking it. Explaining this without epicycles was one of the first big wins for the Sun-centred model.
